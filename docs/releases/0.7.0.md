# Nextcalibur 0.7.0

Four modes built from what Windows chose for the machine, ceilings that can
be lowered and tested, and the machine's power read rather than assumed.

## New

- **Four modes: Power Saver, Balanced (Office), Gaming, Performance**
  (Güç Tasarrufu, Dengeli (Ofis), Oyun, Performans). Balanced runs on the
  firmware's Office profile with Windows' balanced plan and power mode; Power
  Saver at Windows' best power efficiency; each mode a step apart. The System
  page says what each mode is for. A fresh install starts on Balanced.
- **Each mode's plan from Windows' own plans on the machine**, read at every
  start: Power Saver from Power saver (never less efficient than Balanced),
  Balanced from Balanced, Gaming between Balanced and High performance,
  Performance from High performance without its pinned floor.
- **Power Saver at 90%.** Any ceiling under 100% turns the boost off; at full
  load, measured: 70% 1.53 GHz 53 °C, 90% 2.01 GHz 60 °C, 100% 3.57 GHz 97 °C.
- **Processor ceilings** per mode in Settings, never above the measured upper
  bounds: Power Saver as a percentage, the others as a clock limit with the
  boost kept up to it.
- **Ceiling test** per mode: full load from the ceiling in force down until
  the mode's temperature holds (65 / 75 / 85 / 90 °C); suggested when cooling
  was needed three times in a week. It will not start during an update, a
  driver, firmware or BIOS install, maintenance or a restore point; what runs
  is listed live in four groups (downloads apart) and closed only after a
  ticked consent; services are started again after; everything is logged.
- **The mode on battery** is a choice: Power Saver, Balanced or leave it.
- **Plans watched**: a change from outside to what Nextcalibur wrote is told,
  with a button to put it back; on another plan the automatic parts stand down.
- **A restore point before Nextcalibur's first changes**, with a record of
  what the machine had been through (fresh Windows, the vendor's software
  now or before, an earlier Nextcalibur).
- **Power definitions** on the Power page: the processor's base power, limits,
  throttle point and top clock, the graphics card's limit, the battery's
  capacity - each with its maker's definition and who set the value in force
  (here: a 65 W limit set by Casper's firmware, Intel's definition 45 W).
- **The firmware's own tables (SMBIOS)** read at every start; a BIOS change
  since the last start is logged and audited.
- **Temperature from two sources**: the processor's own register for the
  early climb, the embedded controller as the reference. On the recorded full
  load, 90 °C was seen at once instead of 17-25 seconds later.
- **USB and Wi-Fi power saving** and **NVIDIA's power management mode** in
  Settings - shown as Windows and the driver have them, changed only by you.
- **Programs busy at idle**: a scan, and Windows' Efficiency mode for the ones
  you pick.
- **The keyboard lighting comes back at start** as it was left, the Fn+Space
  step included.
- **Win11Debloat** for the ads inside Windows (someone else's tool, asked first).
- **Diagnostics**: `nextcalibur heat`, `limits`, `plans`, `firmware`,
  `preflight`, `nvpower`.

## Fixed

- **Drop-down lists were unreadable in the dark theme** (white on white).
- **Gaming's idle heat**: its energy preference, set eagerer than Windows'
  Balanced in 0.6.2, held cores at 4.4 GHz at idle.
